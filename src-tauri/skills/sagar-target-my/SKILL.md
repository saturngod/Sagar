# Myanmar (Burmese) target-language rules

Write Myanmar that a Burmese reader finds natural and easy to read. The Book Settings glossary always overrides these rules.

## Encoding
- Use standard Myanmar Unicode only (never Zawgyi). Keep correct character order for vowels, medials, asat and tone marks.

## Register
- Narration: choose one style for the whole book and keep it. Literary written style uses သည်၊ ၏၊ ၌၊ ၍၊ သော; spoken style uses တယ်၊ ရဲ့၊ မှာ၊ ပြီး၊ တဲ့. Follow the source's formality: novels may use spoken-style narration, formal non-fiction normally uses literary style. Never mix both styles in the same narration.
- Dialogue: always use natural spoken style (တယ်၊ ပါ၊ နော်၊ လေ၊ ဗျာ၊ ရှင်), matched to the speaker.

## Pronouns and politeness
- Choose pronouns by speaker gender, age and relationship: ကျွန်တော် (polite I, male), ကျွန်မ (polite I, female), ငါ (intimate or rough I), နင် / မင်း (intimate or rough you), ခင်ဗျား (polite you from a male speaker), ရှင် (polite you from a female speaker). Keep each character's choice consistent unless the relationship changes.
- Use ပါ and polite endings to show respect where the source is polite.

## Names and honorifics
- Burmese honorifics before names: ဦး (adult man), ဒေါ် (adult woman), ကို (young man), မ (young woman), မောင် (boy or younger man). Use them only where they fit the source's politeness; do not add them to foreign names unless the glossary does.
- Write foreign names by sound in Myanmar script, consistently every time. Use the established Myanmar spelling for well-known countries, cities and people.
- Chinese names follow Mandarin pronunciation, written consistently in Myanmar script.

## Numbers and punctuation
- In running prose use Myanmar digits (၀၁၂၃၄၅၆၇၈၉) consistently; keep Western digits inside codes, URLs, formulas and system/stat panels.
- End sentences with ။ and use ၊ for pauses between clauses. Do not use the Latin full stop in Myanmar prose.
- Keep quotation marks, question and exclamation marks as in the source.

## Style
- Prefer natural Myanmar word order and idiom over English sentence structure. Avoid long chains of nested clauses; split meaning across clauses naturally while keeping the same paragraph.
