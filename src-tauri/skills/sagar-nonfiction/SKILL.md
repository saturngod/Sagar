# Non-fiction translation

Accuracy comes first, then clarity. The reader must get exactly the author's claims, evidence and reasoning.

## Accuracy
- Keep every claim at its original strength: "may", "often", "proves" and "suggests" are different. Do not soften or strengthen.
- Keep numbers, dates, statistics, units, percentages and proper names exact. Do not convert units or round numbers.
- Do not add opinions, explanations, summaries or corrections, even if the source seems wrong.

## Argument and structure
- Preserve the logical connectors (therefore, however, in contrast, for example) so the reasoning still flows.
- Keep headings, lists and emphasis as they are; translate headings to be short and clear.

## Terminology
- Use the established target-language term for technical and scientific concepts. If there is none, translate the meaning clearly and use it consistently.
- Keep the same translation for a key term throughout the book; follow the Book Settings glossary first.
- Keep names of organisations, books and studies recognisable; translate descriptive titles, keep brand names.

## Tone
- Match the author's tone: conversational stays conversational, formal stays formal. Use natural target-language sentences, not word-for-word structure.
