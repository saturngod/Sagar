# Danmei (耽美) translation

Danmei novels centre on the romance between two male leads. The setting is often xianxia, wuxia, historical or modern; apply the matching world rules below together with the relationship rules.

## The relationship
- The emotional development between the leads is the core of the story. Keep tension, teasing, longing, jealousy and tenderness at the same intensity as the source.
- Translate romantic and intimate scenes faithfully. Do not soften, censor, skip or make them more explicit than the source.

## Forms of address
- How the leads address each other shows closeness and power: 师尊 (honoured master), 师兄 (senior brother), 哥哥/哥 (gege, older brother, also affectionate), 阿 + name (A-name, intimate), full name (cold or angry), nicknames and courtesy names (字). Keep each form distinct and translate it consistently.
- When a character switches form of address, keep the switch visible: it is usually a key emotional moment.
- Keep pronouns as in the source; do not change any character's gender.
- Roles such as 攻 (top) and 受 (bottom) are fan terms; they appear in author notes, not usually in the story. Translate them only where they appear.

## Settings
- In xianxia or wuxia settings, keep cultivation realms, sect ranks, martial techniques and 江湖 terms consistent, the same way as a xianxia or wuxia novel.
- In historical settings, keep court titles (陛下, 殿下, 王爷, 将军) and polite old-fashioned speech. In modern settings, use natural modern speech.

## Shared Chinese web-novel rules
- Chapter headings such as 第十二章 + title: translate the number and title naturally in the target language, keeping the same heading element.
- Four-character idioms (成语) and set phrases: translate the meaning and image, not each character. Keep them short and vivid.
- Keep the short paragraphs, one-line reactions and cliffhanger chapter endings. Do not merge lines.
- Sound-effect words (砰, 轰, 嗡) and onomatopoeia: use a natural target-language sound word.
- Author notes and chapter-end messages are part of the source: translate them as notes, separate from the story.
- Consistency: each name, title, realm, technique, sect, artifact and place is translated exactly the same way every time. Follow the Book Settings glossary first; it overrides everything here.
- For each type of term, use one rule for the whole book: either write it by its Mandarin sound in the target script, or translate its meaning. Do not mix the two for the same type.
- People names are written by Mandarin sound (not translated by meaning) unless the glossary says otherwise. Keep the family-name-first order.
- Do not add footnotes, pinyin in brackets or explanations inside the text.
