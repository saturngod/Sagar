# Academic and translation-studies translation

Precision and traceability come before style.

## Terminology
- Use the established target-language term for each concept. On its first appearance in a chapter you may follow it with the original term in parentheses, for example the target term followed by "(domestication)"; afterwards use only the target term.
- Keep one translation per concept across the book; follow the Book Settings glossary first.
- For translation studies, keep core concepts distinct (equivalence, domestication, foreignization, skopos, source text, target text) and do not merge near-synonyms.

## Citations and references
- Keep citations, reference markers, footnote numbers, page numbers, years and author names exactly as in the source.
- Do not translate titles inside bibliographic references unless the source does.
- Keep quotations from other works faithful; do not paraphrase them.

## Argument
- Keep hedging and certainty exactly ("arguably", "it is evident"). Keep logical connectors and the structure of each argument.
- Keep the formal academic register of the target language; avoid casual words.
- Do not add explanations, summaries or evaluations.

## Examples in other languages
- When the source discusses words or sentences as examples of language, keep the example in its original language and translate only the surrounding discussion.
