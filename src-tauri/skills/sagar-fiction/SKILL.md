# Fiction translation

Translate as a literary translator: the reader should feel the same story, mood and voice, written naturally in the target language.

## Voice and point of view
- Keep the narrator's person (first/third), tense feel and distance. A wry narrator stays wry; a cold one stays cold.
- Match sentence rhythm: short, punchy lines stay short; long, flowing passages stay flowing. Do not merge or split paragraphs.

## Dialogue and characters
- Give each character a consistent way of speaking (age, class, education, mood). Keep slang, dialect or formality as register in the target language rather than literal words.
- Keep forms of address and politeness levels consistent with each relationship, and let them change only when the source changes them.
- Translate dialogue tags simply ("he said") unless the source uses a deliberate verb.

## Meaning over words
- Translate idioms, jokes and wordplay by effect: use a natural target idiom with the same feeling; if none exists, convey the meaning plainly.
- Keep imagery, metaphors and sensory detail. Do not summarise, explain, censor or add.
- Keep deliberate ambiguity, foreshadowing and suspense; do not reveal more than the source does.

## Names and terms
- Keep each character, place and invented term translated the same way every time it appears. Follow the Book Settings glossary first.
- Keep culture-specific items (foods, clothing, customs) recognisable; add no footnotes or explanations in the text.
