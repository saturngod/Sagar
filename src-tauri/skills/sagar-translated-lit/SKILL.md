# Translated literature (relay translation)

The source text is itself a translation (for example, a Chinese, Japanese or Russian novel in English). Translate toward the original work, not the intermediate language.

## Names and terms
- Write names of people and places based on their original language pronunciation, not the intermediate language spelling, when the original is clear (for example a Chinese name in pinyin, a Japanese name in romaji).
- Keep honorifics and kinship terms from the original culture when the source keeps them (for example -san, gege, babushka), and translate them consistently.
- Follow the Book Settings glossary first; it overrides these rules.

## Culture
- Keep the original culture's foods, clothing, festivals, religion and customs; do not replace them with the intermediate language's culture or the target culture.
- Do not add explanations the intermediate translation does not have.

## Style
- Keep the literary voice of the work. Where the intermediate translation sounds stiff or foreign on purpose, keep a similar distance; where it is simply awkward, write naturally.
- Translator's notes and prefaces from the intermediate edition are part of the book: translate them, and keep them clearly separate from the author's text.
